## International RSE Day Celebration

On 8 October 2026, Research Software Engineers around the world will come together for the sixth [International RSE Day](https://researchsoftware.org/council/intl-rse-day.html), a global celebration of the people who build, maintain and support research software. Communities around the world will mark the occasion with local events, bringing RSEs together to connect, exchange experiences and share ideas.

As part of this international celebration, NL-RSE is organising a local event for the Dutch RSE community. Our aim is to create an inspiring and welcoming space where RSEs from across the Netherlands can meet, connect and learn from one another.

Whether you are an experienced RSE, just starting out in the field, or simply interested in research software and the people behind it, we invite you to join us and celebrate International RSE Day together with the Dutch RSE community.

### SuperCode project: Enhancing SLMs for Sustainable Code Optimization in Radio-Astronomy
In radio astronomy, computational requirements scale dramatically with the resolution and sensitivity of the instrument. The current state-of-the-art telescopes can barely be processed with conventional digital technologies, but this may not be the case for future instruments. Furthermore, the climate crisis requires us to carefully consider the environmental impact of such instruments. 

We investigate the use of LLMs to generate and optimize code for large-scale scientific instruments and processing, focusing on radio astronomy and sustainability. We need to optimize existing codes or port them to accelerators, while simultaneously making sure that the optimization process itself is also energy efficient. This poses a challenge, since LLMs are energy-intensive, we therefore propose to use Small Language Models (SLMs) instead to limit environmental impact. 

We extend the SLMs in two complementary ways to improve code generation quality and performance: first with a multi-sampling generation strategy and second with incorporating compiler feedback. We demonstrate that multi-sampling SLMs can match or surpass larger single-generation models with fewer computational resources. We also show that feeding compiler output back into the SLMs leads to consistent improvements across all tested models.

<p style="color: #650045; font-style: italic;">Elisa Chiarotto is a first-year PhD candidate at the University of Leiden, with a background in theoretical physics. Her main research interests include machine learning, particularly deep neural networks, particle physics, and astrophysics.</p>
