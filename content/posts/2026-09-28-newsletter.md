Welcome to the September 2026 NL-RSE Community Newsletter! As summer winds down and the first signs of autumn appear, September brings a feeling of new beginnings: whether you are heading back to campus after the summer break, starting a new project or simply getting back into the rhythm of things.   

There is plenty to catch up on across the Dutch research software community. One date to mark in your calendar: **International RSE Day on 8 October**, which we'll be celebrating with a special NL-RSE event. Read on to find out more!

## Dates for your calendar

**International RSE Day – NL-RSE Community Meet-up**   
**8 October @ Amsterdam**   
This year's International RSE Day takes place on Thursday 8 October and NL-RSE is organizing a community meetup to celebrate! Join us for an afternoon of technical and community-focused talks, including updates from various RSE professionalisation working groups and a special guest presentation by Jannetta Steyn, senior RSE at Newcastle University. The meet-up will take place at Amsterdam Science Park from 13:00–16:00. Checkout the programme and register <a href="https://nl-rse.org/events/2026-10-08-meetup/" style="color: #650045; font-weight: bold; font-style: italic;">here</a>.   

**FORRT AI in Metascience Conference**   
**28–30 September @ online**   
How is AI changing the way research is done, checked and understood? The FORRT AI in Metascience Online Conference brings together researchers and practitioners for three days of talks and workshops exploring AI in the research pipeline, evidence synthesis, research evaluation, replication and reproducibility. The conference is free to attend and takes place entirely online. Find out more and register <a href="https://forrt.org/ai-metascience/" style="color: #650045; font-weight: bold; font-style: italic;">here</a>.   

**Deadline: DUCOMS Day 2026 submissions**   
**30 September**   
Want to share your work at this year’s Dutch Computational Science Day (DUCOMS)? The deadline to submit an abstract is 30 September. DUCOMS brings together computational science professionals from academia and industry to exchange knowledge and ideas across disciplines. The event itself will take place on 30 November in Utrecht. Find out more and submit your abstract <a href="https://www.computationalsciencenl.nl/en/ducoms-day-2026/" style="color: #650045; font-weight: bold; font-style: italic;">here</a>.   

**Deadline: Institute for Research Software Fellowship Programme applications**   
**5 October**   
Interested in becoming an advocate for good research software practice? Applications for the 2027 Institute for Research Software Fellowship Programme are open until 5 October. Fellows receive a £4,000 budget, mentoring and opportunities to connect with a wider community. The programme is open to applicants at any career stage and from a wide range of backgrounds. Find out more and apply <a href="https://www.software.ac.uk/programmes/fellowship-programme/apply-fellowship-programme" style="color: #650045; font-weight: bold; font-style: italic;">here</a>.   

**Research Data Alliance Plenary Meeting (P27)**   
**6–8 October @ London, UK**   
The Research Data Alliance (RDA) will bring its global research data community together in London for its 27th Plenary Meeting. Under the theme “Shaping a global open research commons”, P27 will explore how researchers and organisations can build a strong, connected and sustainable open research ecosystem, with a focus on the data, tools, infrastructure and skills needed to support modern research. Find out more and register <a href="https://www.rd-alliance.org/plenaries/p27/" style="color: #650045; font-weight: bold; font-style: italic;">here</a>.   

**2nd Symposium on Accelerated Computing for Application Workflows**   
**7 October @ Utrecht**   
Want to speed up your scientific applications or find out what GPU acceleration could do for your project? SURF and the Netherlands eScience Center are bringing researchers, developers and tool builders together for a day focused on accelerating scientific workflows. Find out more and register on the <a href="https://www.surf.nl/en/agenda/2nd-symposium-on-accelerated-computing-for-application-workflows" style="color: #650045; font-weight: bold; font-style: italic;">event website</a>.   

**Institute of Open Science Practices (IOSP) 2026**   
**12–15 October @ Leiden**   
The third edition of the Institute of Open Science Practices (IOSP) will bring together people building, managing and using open science infrastructure for four days of hands-on workshops, collaboration and community activities. This year's programme explores topics including resilient data infrastructure, modular research components and open protocols. Find out more about the programme and ways to participate <a href="https://iosp.science/workshops" style="color: #650045; font-weight: bold; font-style: italic;">here</a>.   

**Deadline: Lorentz Competition 2026 applications**   
**25 October**   
The Netherlands eScience Center and the Lorentz Center are inviting researchers to take part in the annual eScience Center–Lorentz Competition. This year's competition focuses on digital sovereignty in research. The winner will organise a workshop at the Lorentz Center in Leiden and receive research software engineering support from the Netherlands eScience Center. Find out more and apply <a href="https://www.esciencecenter.nl/calls-for-proposals/escience-center-lorentz-competition/" style="color: #650045; font-weight: bold; font-style: italic;">here</a>.   

**Deadline: Belgian Research Software Engineering Day submissions**   
**26 October**   
The 4th Belgian Research Software Engineering Day will take place on **15 December in Ghent** and contributions are now welcome. Topics include software engineering practices, FAIR and reproducible software, LLMs, HPC and computational challenges in science. Submit your contribution by 26 October via the <a href="https://www.vibconferences.be/events/research-software-engineering-day-4th-edition" style="color: #650045; font-weight: bold; font-style: italic;">event website</a>.


## News

**National Research Software Day 2027 announced**   
The next National Research Software Day has been confirmed for **20 April 2027**, with **Utrecht University** as the host. Following the success of the previous editions, the event will once again bring together the Dutch research software community for a day of sharing, learning and connecting. More information will be announced in due course, but you can already save the date and find out more <a href="https://nl-rse.org/pages/nrsd-2027" style="color: #650045; font-weight: bold; font-style: italic;">here</a>.   

**Software Sustainability Institute becomes the Institute for Research Software**   
The Software Sustainability Institute (SSI) has a new name: Institute for Research Software (IRS). The new identity reflects the growth of the research software field and the Institute's evolving role in supporting research software and the people who develop and maintain it. The Institute says its purpose remains unchanged: Better Software, Better Research. Read more about the change in their <a href="https://www.software.ac.uk/blog/introducing-institute-research-software" style="color: #650045; font-weight: bold; font-style: italic;">announcement</a>.   

**Open Science NL opens FAIR Research Outputs call**   
A new Open Science NL call is now open for FAIR Research Outputs. The call supports initiatives that help make research outputs more FAIR, strengthening the infrastructure and practices needed to share and reuse research. This is the first round of the call, with applications now open. Find out more about the funding and eligibility <a href="https://www.openscience.nl/en/news/first-round-of-the-fair-research-outputs-call-now-open" style="color: #650045; font-weight: bold; font-style: italic;">here</a>.   

**New mobility scheme supports Open Science professionals**   
Open Science NL has launched a new mobility scheme to support the professional development of people working in Open Science. The scheme provides opportunities for professionals to spend time at another organisation, learn from colleagues and build connections across the Open Science community. <a href="https://www.openscience.nl/en/news/new-call-gives-a-boost-to-the-professionalisation-of-open-science-professionals" style="color: #650045; font-weight: bold; font-style: italic;">Read the full announcement</a> to find out more about the scheme and how to apply.   

## Blog posts, tools & more

**RSE Survey at the Netherlands eScience Center**   
What does the RSE role look like in practice, and how do RSEs see their work and professional development? The Netherlands eScience Center recently surveyed RSEs to better understand their roles, backgrounds, activities and challenges. The results offer an interesting snapshot of the Dutch RSE community and highlight some of the similarities and differences in how RSEs work. Read the <a href="https://blog.esciencecenter.nl/posts/2026-08-27---rse-survey/" style="color: #650045; font-weight: bold; font-style: italic;">survey results</a> to find out more.   

**Fortitude: a linter for Fortran**   
Fortran is still widely used in scientific and high-performance computing, but keeping large or legacy codebases clean and consistent can be a challenge. Fortitude is a modern linter for Fortran that checks code for common errors, potential problems and style issues, helping developers catch issues early and maintain healthier codebases. Have a look at the <a href="https://fortitude.readthedocs.io/en/stable/" style="color: #650045; font-weight: bold; font-style: italic;">Fortitude documentation</a> to see what it can do.   

**Moving away from GraphPad Prism: a practical migration guide**   
Thinking about moving your analysis workflows away from GraphPad Prism? This <a href="https://plotwright.furiga.com/coming-from-prism.html?utm_source=nl_rse&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=research_software_guides" style="color: #650045; font-weight: bold; font-style: italic;">practical migration guide</a> maps out which data, analysis and figure workflows can be transferred, what may not transfer cleanly, and points to free alternatives such as JASP and R. The guide is a developer submission from Furiga, the company behind Plotwright, a paid browser-based tool; the guide itself is publicly available and does not require an account.
